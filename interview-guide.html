One-minute project explanation

Use this when the interviewer asks, “Tell me about your project.”

The system receives a support ticket through Streamlit and FastAPI. It stores the ticket in PostgreSQL, uses LangChain and an LLM to classify the category, urgency, and intent, retrieves related FAQ content from ChromaDB, and generates a source-grounded draft response. A validation step checks whether the draft is supported by the FAQ. Safe tickets can be answered automatically, while refunds, payments, security issues, critical tickets, and uncertain answers are sent to a human agent. The agent can approve, edit, or reject the draft, and only the approved response becomes the final response.

12 important interview questions

Question 01

Explain the complete ticket flow.

A customer submits a subject and description. FastAPI validates the request and stores the original ticket in PostgreSQL. The classifier predicts category, urgency, and intent. The retriever searches ChromaDB for relevant FAQ sections. LangChain sends the ticket and retrieved context to the LLM, which generates a draft. A validator checks whether the draft is grounded. Finally, escalation rules either allow an automatic response or send the draft to a human agent.

Question 02

Where does LangChain fit?

LangChain is the orchestration layer. It connects prompts to the LLM, requests structured outputs, connects retrieval context to the generation prompt, and runs the validation chain. It does not replace FastAPI, PostgreSQL, or ChromaDB. The LLM performs language understanding; LangChain organizes the calls and data flow.

Ticket → PromptTemplate → ChatOpenAI → ClassificationResult Ticket → Retriever → FAQ context → PromptTemplate → ChatOpenAI → Draft Draft + FAQ context → Validation prompt → ValidationResult
Question 03

How does classification work?

The classifier receives the subject and description. A prompt defines the allowed categories and urgency values. The LLM returns structured JSON containing category, urgency, intent, confidence, and summary. Pydantic validates that output. Deterministic business rules then escalate sensitive intents such as refunds, payments, security incidents, privacy requests, legal questions, and complaints.

Input: “I was charged twice and want a refund.” Output: { "category": "billing", "urgency": "high", "intent": "duplicate_charge", "confidence": 0.94 }
Question 04

What is RAG?

RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Instead of asking the LLM to answer from general knowledge, the system first retrieves relevant company FAQ content and gives it to the LLM as context. The model then drafts an answer using that context. This reduces hallucinations and makes the response traceable to company documents.

Question 05

What does ChromaDB store?

ChromaDB stores vector representations of FAQ sections and their metadata. Each FAQ section becomes a searchable document containing the source filename, section name, content, and embedding. When a ticket arrives, its text is compared with those vectors and the most semantically similar sections are returned.

Question 06

Who validates the answer?

There are two layers. First, an AI validation chain checks whether the generated answer is supported by the retrieved FAQ and returns supported, confidence, and unsupported claims. Second, a human agent is the final authority for refunds, payments, security, complaints, critical cases, and uncertain answers. AI validation supports the workflow; it does not replace human approval for risky cases.

Important: generation creates a draft; validation checks the draft; the escalation policy decides whether human approval is required.
Question 07

Why are refunds sent to a human?

A refund can have financial consequences and may require payment verification, policy interpretation, or fraud checks. The AI can summarize the issue and draft a response, but it should not promise or approve a refund. The ticket is therefore placed in waiting_for_review until a support agent makes the final decision.

Question 08

What is the difference between draft and final response?

draft_response is generated by the AI and may still need review. final_response is the message approved for the customer. For safe tickets, the draft can become final automatically. For sensitive tickets, final_response remains empty until an agent approves or edits the draft.

Question 09

What happens when no FAQ matches?

The retriever returns no useful context or a score below the configured threshold. The validator marks the answer as unsupported or low-confidence. The escalation policy then sets the ticket to waiting_for_review with a reason such as no_relevant_faq. The system must not invent an answer from missing knowledge.

No verified context → no automatic answer → human review.
Question 10

How do approve, edit, and reject actions work?

The agent sees pending tickets in the Streamlit review page. Approve copies the AI draft into final_response. Edit stores the agent’s rewritten response as final_response. Reject leaves final_response empty and keeps the ticket in review. Each action is recorded in the reviews table with reviewer ID, action, comments, and timestamp.

Question 11

Why use PostgreSQL and ChromaDB separately?

They solve different problems. PostgreSQL is the source of truth for structured business data: tickets, statuses, reviews, responses, and audit history. ChromaDB is optimized for semantic similarity search over unstructured FAQ content. PostgreSQL answers “What is the ticket’s current status?” while ChromaDB answers “Which FAQ content is most relevant?”

Question 12

What are the limitations of the current prototype?

The prototype uses synchronous processing, demo roles instead of full authentication, seeded FAQ documents, and mock AI fallback. The mock validator is simplified, so production should use stronger grounding checks and evaluation. It also needs real email or CRM integration, background workers, attachment scanning, rate limiting, PII masking, model monitoring, database migrations, and a larger labeled evaluation dataset.

Good improvement roadmap
  • Add JWT authentication and role-based access.
  • Move processing to Celery or a queue.
  • Add independent answer evaluation and regression tests.
  • Integrate email, Zendesk, or Salesforce.
  • Add FAQ versioning and admin upload workflow.
  • Measure classification accuracy, grounded-answer rate, escalation rate, and human correction rate.

Final interview summary

The project follows a controlled AI workflow: the LLM proposes a classification and response, retrieval provides company-specific evidence, validation checks grounding, deterministic rules identify risk, and a human agent controls sensitive outcomes. This design makes the system useful while preventing unsupported or financially risky answers from being sent automatically.

Memorize this line: “AI drafts, retrieval grounds the answer, rules decide whether automation is safe, and humans approve sensitive cases.”
